 1. Purpose, problem and success definition
 Purpose. Define what FloodLens AI shall do, how its modules exchange information, what the two user groups
 can access, how failure and uncertainty are handled, and what must be demonstrated during the hackathon.

 Original assigned problem. For the Nepal Terai, compare suitable pre-flood and post-flood optical satellite
 imagery, detect newly inundated land using spectral water indices, produce a georeferenced flood extent map
 and identify affected towns/villages. Evaluate flood detection and settlement flags against reliable reference
 data where available.

 Proposed innovation. Extend that observed-flood pipeline with time-stamped flood-risk estimates before
 events, human-reviewed citizen warnings and location guidance, and an evidence-based comparison/retraining
 process after events.


    North-star demonstration: One historical flood replay that connects a stored risk estimate, an authority-reviewed warning, an
    Android notification, optical satellite mapping, settlement/asset exposure, a citizen assistance request, and
    prediction-versus-observation evaluation.

 6.1 Accessibility and device behaviour
 • Use readable text, high-contrast risk indicators and accessible labels; never rely on color alone.

 • Support English-first prototype; plan Nepal-localized language content only after human review and
 translation verification.

 • Require explicit location permission and allow manual area selection; do not show other citizens' precise
 locations.

 • When offline, clearly label cached hazards and the last successful sync; avoid claiming current safety from
 stale data.

 • Use REST refresh on reconnect; push notifications are best-effort and do not guarantee emergency delivery.

 7.4 Realtime and security requirements
 • Persist before publish: save alerts and requests to the database before sending WebSocket notifications.

 • Authentication: validate sessions, authorize channels by user role/location, use WSS and protected APIs.

 • Reconnect: clients reconnect with backoff, then call REST to reconcile missed updates.

 • Scale boundary: in-process broadcaster is acceptable for one-instance demo; multi-instance requires shared
 pub/sub or broker.

 • Minimal data: do not broadcast private assistance narratives or precise user coordinates to every connected
 client.

 10. End-to-end workflows and responsibilities
 10.1 Workflow A - risk forecast to approved warning
 1. Import available weather, river, release and terrain inputs; validate timestamps and health.

 2. Generate +1h/+3h/+6h risk layers and exposed-asset intersections, including uncertainty.

 3. Persist prediction and create an alert draft when documented conditions trigger review.

 4. Authorized officer checks evidence, edits target area/content and approves or rejects.

 5. If approved, save alert record, notify connected clients via WS and test Android push; log deliveries.

 6. Citizen app fetches current advisory, destination list and known hazard/route information.

 10.2 Workflow B - observed flood to impact report
 1. Choose pre-event and post-event optical satellite scenes for one Nepal Terai sector.

 2. Process cloud/quality masks, compute water indices and derive newly flooded raster/vector.

 3. Validate extent against reference imagery/mask where obtainable, reporting accuracy and limitations.

 4. Overlay settlement and asset geometries; calculate inundated area and potential exposures.

 5. Publish flood layer and downloadable report; linked authorities can inspect affected regions.

 6. Citizen assistance requests enter authority review queue, with updates to submitting users.

 10.3 Workflow C - historical evaluation and model improvement
 1. Retrieve archived issued predictions and suitably time-matched verified observations.

 2. Compute appropriate spatial / forecast metrics and mark unavailable evaluations inconclusive.

 3. Review data errors and wrong predictions without masking missed events.

 4. If enough verified examples exist, train a candidate offline on training events only.

 5. Compare candidate with baseline on held-out events; human reviewer decides whether to approve.

 13. Version 1 boundary and exclusions
 13.1 Must deliver by feature freeze (Day 5)
 • Assigned optical pre/post satellite mapping, flood polygon/raster, settlement flags and impact report for a
 real scene.

 • One evidence-backed risk baseline with three time horizons; risk map and exposed-asset assessment.

 • Working authority web dashboard, citizen Android core screens, common FastAPI/PostGIS backend, REST and
 WS.

 • Approved demo push warning, citizen assistance workflow, reproducible historical replay and documented
 accuracy analysis.

 13.2 Retained but conditional / experimental
 3D web terrain and optimized Android view; dam-release/river sensor adapters; inundation-depth modelling;
 Sentinel-1 radar; verified shelters/hospitals; limited route guidance; test SMS/WhatsApp integrations; candidate
 model training. These remain in the design, but a module is only labelled working after passing the
 corresponding data and test gate.

 13.3 Explicitly excluded from operational claims
 • No guaranteed 6-7-hour lead time, exact flood arrival time, accurate depth at any address or universal flood
 prediction.

 • No automated unaudited public emergency broadcast, guaranteed message delivery or real rescue dispatch.

 • No confirmed structural destruction solely from remote-sensed floodwater.

 • No guaranteed safe evacuation route/hospital/shelter from elevation or incomplete road data.

 • No autonomous deployment of newly retrained models and no nationwide production emergency
 certification.
